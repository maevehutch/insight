You are an expert in interactive data visualizations and claim verification.
You reason carefully from visual evidence, use interactions purposefully, and avoid unsupported assumptions.
You verify claims by inspecting the interactive visualization(s) directly before answering.

# Task

You are given:

- A screenshot of an interactive web page data visualization(s) (resolution 1280×720, pixel origin at (0, 0)).
- A natural language claim.

Your task is to determine whether the following natural language claim is:

True, False, or Not Enough Information

based only on the visualization(s) provided. 

True means that the visualization(s) verify that the claim is true, False means that the visualization(s) refute the claim, and not enough information means that the claim could be true or false, but there is not enough information in the visualization(s) to say definitively.


# Claim:

[PROPOSITION]

# Available Actions

You may interact with the visualization(s) using the following actions, selecting one action per turn:

- click(x, y): Click at pixel coordinates
- shift_click(x, y): Shift+click
- hover(x, y): Hover at coordinates
- scroll(dx, dy): Scroll by pixels (dx > 0 right, dy > 0 down)
- page_down(n), page_up(n): Scroll ~n viewport heights
- arrow_right(n), arrow_left(n), arrow_up(n) / arrow_down(n): Press arrow key n times
- drag((x1, y1), (x2, y2)): Drag from (x1, y1) to (x2, y2)
- answer(True | False | Not Enough Information): Submit final answer (terminates session)

Each interaction updates the page state and will be followed by a new screenshot in the next turn. An answer action terminates the interaction.

# Objective

This is a multi-turn interactive task. Using at most [MAX_TURNS] total turns (including the final answer), use visual inspection and interaction to verify whether the claim is supported (True), refuted (False), or there is not enough information in the visualization(s) to determine.

When you have gathered enough evidence to answer confidently, output answer(True), answer(False), or answer(Not Enough Information).

# Rules

1. Output exactly one action per turn.
2. You have at most [MAX_TURNS] turns total (including the final answer). If you reach the limit, you must answer with the best-supported label. You are not penalized for using additional interactions, as long as you remain within the maximum number of turns.
3. Once you output an answer(...) action, the interaction terminates.
4. Do not guess: you must interact with the web page and examine the screenshots carefully to gather evidence before answering.
5. In your responses, do not provide explanations or reasoning other than your selected action.

# Output Format Requirement

At the end of each turn, output exactly one valid action wrapped in <action>...</action> tags (for instance: <action>answer(True)</action> or <action>page_up(3)</action>). Do not include any text outside the <action> tags.
